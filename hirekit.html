HireKit

A team-built tool that turns one Google Form submission into a complete hiring kit — job description, interview loop, interview questions, and TA sourcing notes — in under a minute.

Google Apps Script Google Forms + Sheets Google Docs + Drive Gemini via LiteLLM Gmail
The problem

JD quality and interview preparation in our TA function varied depending on who wrote them, how much time they had, and how well the intake conversation went. Writing a JD from scratch could take days. Interview questions were built inconsistently. Sourcing approaches weren't documented. The output depended too much on the individual.

HireKit was a team effort to solve that inconsistency problem — to standardise the starting point without removing the recruiter from the process. TA still owns the intake, the relationship, and the final JD. HireKit removes the blank-page problem and gives the team a consistent, structured foundation to work from every time.

What it produces

One form submission. One Google Doc. Everything the TA team needs to start a new hire process.

01
Full job description
About the Role, What You Will Do, What You Will Need, Work Arrangement — written in job ad tone, adjusted to the job level, with AI expectations embedded naturally.
02
Interview loop table
4 rounds pre-filled with suggested interviewers, focus areas, and format. Recruiter → HM → Cross-functional → Senior leader. Editable before use.
03
Interview question guide
Three sections: Hiring Manager questions, AI Capabilities and Mindset, Culture and Collaboration. Tailored to role and seniority. Conversational, not textbook.
04
TA sourcing notes
Internal only — not shared with the HM. Suggested talent pools, search titles, and candidate considerations. Fully dynamic based on role, level, market, and brand.
The doc is automatically named, moved to the correct brand folder in Drive, shared with the HM and TA Partner, and an email notification goes out with the link. Brand boilerplate is selected automatically based on the HM's input.
How it works
1
HM submits the form
The Hiring Manager fills in one Google Form — job title, level, location, brand, responsibilities, must-haves, and a few fields that shape the AI output. Once submitted, they're done.
trigger: onFormSubmit → handleFormSubmit()
2
Three AI calls are made
The script sends the HM's inputs to Gemini via LiteLLM three times — once for the JD, once for interview questions, once for TA sourcing notes. Each call uses a detailed prompt encoding the recruiting logic: tone rules, seniority logic, scope inference, formatting constraints, and AI-first mandates.
model: gemini-2.5-flash-lite · max tokens: 900 per call
3
Google Doc is assembled
A new Doc is created, named by job title, country, and date. Brand boilerplate is added first, then the JD, interview loop table, interview questions, intake placeholder, and sourcing notes — all formatted and structured by the script. No manual cleanup needed.
output: one structured Google Doc per submission
4
Sharing, email, and logging
The doc moves to the correct brand folder, gets shared with the HM and TA Partner as editors, and an email goes out with the document link. Three log tabs are written to automatically: automation logs, LLM output review by job level and function, and debug logs for troubleshooting.
logs: JD_Automation_Logs · LLM_Review_By_JobLevel_Function · JD_Debug_Logs

The prompts — where the work actually happened

The code is plumbing. The prompts are the product. Most of the build time went into encoding recruiting expertise into instructions precise enough for the AI to follow consistently.

JD prompt Most iterations
Write like a job ad, not an HR policy — no jargon, varied sentence starters, short active lines
Adjust tone and scope based on job level — JL2 is execution-focused, JL6+ is strategic and cross-functional
Infer group vs market scope from role context, not just location
Include exactly one AI-related line in responsibilities and one in requirements, written naturally
Plain text output only — specific headings, no markdown, short pseudo-bullet lines
Interview question prompt

Conversational questions — not textbook competency-framework language
Senior roles get harder questions on strategic judgement and stakeholder influence

My role on the team

HireKit was a collaborative build. The script architecture, LiteLLM integration, and document assembly logic were a team effort. My focus was on the parts that required recruiting domain knowledge.

What I worked on
Prompt refinement — testing and iterating the JD, interview question, and sourcing note prompts across different job levels and functions
End-to-end testing — running the full workflow with real form inputs and reviewing output quality across edge cases
User acceptance testing with hiring managers — running structured sessions with HMs as the primary users, gathering feedback, and translating it into prompt or flow improvements
Seniority and scope logic — defining and refining the rules for how the AI should adjust tone, depth, and scope based on job level and role type
Iteration tracking — documenting what changed between prompt versions and why, to maintain a clear record of what worked and what didn't
The hardest part of building HireKit wasn't the code — it was figuring out what a good JD actually looks like and writing it down precisely enough for an AI to follow. That's where recruiting expertise turned into a technical input.
Honest limitations

AI output isn't always right on the first run — especially for niche or very senior roles. TA review is still essential. Output quality also depends on how well the HM fills in the form; a vague brief produces a vague JD.

Each AI call is capped at 900 tokens, which can truncate output for complex roles. There's no automated validation to catch incomplete form submissions before the bot runs — that's still a human check.
